## What it is

The **European Central Bank** (ECB) sets monetary policy for the countries that use the euro. Its statute created the ECB and the wider European System of Central Banks from 1 June 1998, and since 1 January 1999 the ECB has been responsible for the euro area's monetary policy ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/gendoc98en.pdf)).

The **Eurosystem** is the ECB plus the national central banks of the euro countries ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/escb/html/index.en.html)). There are 21 euro countries since Bulgaria joined on 1 January 2026 ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr260101~c830245e42.en.html)). It aims for 2% inflation and treats undershooting as just as bad as overshooting ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/strategy/pricestab/html/index.en.html)). For its inflation measure, see the [European Union page](/Macro-Investing-Textbook/part-1-economy/five-big-economies/european-union/).

**Who leads it.** Christine Lagarde has been President since 1 November 2019 ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/decisions/eb/html/executive-board-terms-of-office.en.html)). On 29 October 2026 she announced that she will step down on 7 February 2027, before her eight-year term would have ended in October 2027. The European Council will appoint her successor ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr261029~abbb6dc64b.en.html); [ECB press conference](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/press_conference/monetary-policy-statement/2026/html/ecb.is261029~5c5ffe99e5.en.html)).

## Who decides

Two bodies matter. The **Executive Board** runs the ECB day to day. The **Governing Council** brings the Executive Board together with the governor of each euro country's central bank, and it sets monetary policy.

### The Executive Board

- **Six members:** a President, a Vice-President and four others. The European Council appoints them by qualified majority, on a recommendation from the Council of the EU, after consulting the European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council. They must be nationals of EU countries with "recognised standing and professional experience in monetary or banking matters" ([Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, article 283](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12016E283)).
- **One eight-year term.** "Their term of office shall be eight years and shall not be renewable" ([Treaty, article 283](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12016E283)). So no member ever needs the governments' approval for a second term.
- **What it does.** It prepares Governing Council meetings, carries out monetary policy by instructing the national central banks, and runs the ECB's day-to-day business ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/decisions/eb/html/index.en.html)).

### The Governing Council

- **Who sits on it.** The six Executive Board members plus the governors of the 21 euro area central banks: 27 people in all ([Treaty, article 283](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12016E283)).
- **What it decides.** It formulates monetary policy and adopts the guidelines and decisions needed for the ECB's tasks, including banking supervision decisions made under a non-objection procedure. It usually meets twice a month and takes monetary policy decisions every six weeks, followed by a press conference ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/decisions/govc/html/index.en.html)).
- **How a vote works.** Each member with a voting right has one vote, and a simple majority decides. If the vote is tied, the President has the casting vote. At least two-thirds of the voting members must take part ([EU Council Decision 2003/223/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32003D0223)). In practice, the ECB says most decisions are reached by consensus ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/explainers/tell-me-more/html/voting-rotation.en.html)).

### Permanent and rotating votes

The euro area outgrew one vote per governor. The EU treaties require rotation once there are more than 18 governors, and it started on 1 January 2015, when Lithuania joined ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/explainers/tell-me-more/html/voting-rotation.en.html)). Today 27 people sit at the table but there are 21 votes:

| Who | Members | Votes | Without a vote in a given month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive Board | 6 | 6 (permanent) | None |
| Group 1 governors: Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| Group 2 governors: the other 16 euro countries | 16 | 11 | 5 |
| **Total** | **27** | **21** | **6** |

- **How the groups are set.** Countries are ranked by the size of their economies and their financial sectors. The five largest form the first group ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/explainers/tell-me-more/html/voting-rotation.en.html)).
- **Votes rotate every month.** Governors take turns holding the voting rights in a monthly rotation. All members, with or without a vote that month, attend the meetings and can speak ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/explainers/tell-me-more/html/voting-rotation.en.html)).
- **An example from 2026.** The Bundesbank's governor has no vote in March and August, and the Banca d'Italia's governor has none in January, June and November. A smaller country waits longer: Finland's governor sits out five months of the year ([ECB, 2026 rotation table](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/decisions/govc/html/votingrights.en.html)).

## The tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Deposit facility rate** | The main steering rate. It is what banks earn on overnight deposits at the ECB, and the Governing Council steers its policy stance through it ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me/html/what-is-the-deposit-facility-rate.en.html)). |
| **Main refinancing operations** | The rate at which banks can borrow from the ECB for one week. Since 18 September 2024 it is 15 basis points above the deposit facility rate ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me/html/what-is-the-deposit-facility-rate.en.html)). |
| **Marginal lending facility** | Overnight borrowing at a higher rate ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb-and-you/explainers/tell-me/html/what-is-the-deposit-facility-rate.en.html)). |
| **Other open market operations** | Longer-term refinancing operations, fine-tuning operations and structural operations ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op304~7557c991fb.en.pdf)). |
| **Minimum reserves** | Banks must hold minimum reserves with their national central bank ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op304~7557c991fb.en.pdf)). |
| **Asset purchases** | Used when there is little room left to cut rates ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/glossary/html/act6e.en.html)). |

## Key takeaways

- The ECB runs one monetary policy for **21 countries**. Its Governing Council has 27 members: the six-member Executive Board and the 21 national central bank governors.
- The six Executive Board members, appointed for one **eight-year term**, always vote. The governors share **15 votes** in a monthly rotation: the five largest countries share 4, and the other 16 share 11.
- It decides **every six weeks** and steers policy through the **deposit facility rate**.
- Other tools: the main refinancing rate, the marginal lending facility, longer-term operations, minimum reserves and asset purchases.
