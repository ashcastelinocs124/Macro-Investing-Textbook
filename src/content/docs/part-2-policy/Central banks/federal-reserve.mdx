## What it is

The **Federal Reserve** (the Fed) is the central bank of the United States. Congress created it with the Federal Reserve Act of 1913, and it was built as a central Board with a network of regional Reserve Banks. The 12 Reserve Banks opened for business on 16 November 1914 ([Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/federal-reserve-banks)). The Fed's own history is told in [an overview](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/federal-reserve-history).

The system has three parts: the Board of Governors, the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) and the 12 Reserve Banks ([Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/fed-structure)). Its goals are maximum employment and stable prices, the dual mandate ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/what-economic-goals-does-federal-reserve-seek-to-achieve-through-monetary-policy.htm)). For the goal chart and the indicator it watches, see the [United States page](/Macro-Investing-Textbook/part-1-economy/five-big-economies/united-states/).

**Who leads it.** Kevin Warsh has been Chair since 22 May 2026. His term as Chair runs to 21 May 2030 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/bios/board/warsh.htm)).

## Who decides

Three groups share the work. The **Board of Governors** sits in Washington. The **12 Reserve Banks** each have their own president and board of directors. The **FOMC** brings people from both together to set the policy rate.

### The Board of Governors

- **Seven governors.** The President nominates them and the Senate confirms them. No more than one governor may come from any one of the 12 Federal Reserve districts, and the President must give "due regard to a fair representation" of financial, agricultural, industrial and commercial interests ([Federal Reserve Act, section 10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/section10.htm)).
- **14-year terms, staggered.** One governor's term starts every two years, on 1 February of even-numbered years. So a President normally fills only two seats in a four-year term, unless governors leave early. A governor who serves a full term cannot be reappointed. One who finishes someone else's unexpired term can be ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/bios/board/default.htm)).
- **Chair and vice chairs.** The President also picks, with Senate approval, a Chair, a Vice Chair and a Vice Chair for Supervision from among the governors. Each serves four years in that role ([Federal Reserve Act, section 10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/section10.htm)).
- **The two terms are separate.** Losing the chair does not end a governor's seat. Jerome Powell was Chair from 5 February 2018 to 22 May 2026. He is still a governor, with a term that runs to 31 January 2028 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/bios/board/powell.htm)).
- **Removal.** The President can remove a governor only "for cause" ([Federal Reserve Act, section 10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/section10.htm)).

### The Reserve Banks and their boards of directors

Each of the 12 Reserve Banks is overseen by a board of nine directors in three classes ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/directors/about.htm)):

| Class | Seats | Represents | Chosen by |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 3 | Member banks in the district | Elected by the district's member banks |
| B | 3 | The public | Elected by the district's member banks |
| C | 3 | The public | Appointed by the Board of Governors |

- The Board of Governors picks each board's chair and deputy chair from its Class C directors. Directors serve staggered three-year terms, usually for no more than two full terms ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/directors/about.htm)).
- **Choosing the president.** Only the Class B and C directors choose a Reserve Bank's president. These are the directors who are not tied to a supervised bank. The Board of Governors must approve the choice ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/how-is-a-federal-reserve-bank-president-selected.htm)).
- **Five-year terms.** Every president's term ends at the end of February in years ending in 1 or 6. The Class B and C directors vote on reappointment, and the Board of Governors must approve it ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/what-is-the-process-for-reappointing-reserve-bank-presidents.htm)). So every president's term ended in February 2026, and the next ones end in February 2031.

### How the FOMC votes: permanent and rotating seats

The FOMC meets eight times a year. All 12 Reserve Bank presidents attend every meeting, but only five of them vote ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)).

- **Eight permanent seats.** The seven governors and the president of the New York Fed vote at every meeting.
- **Four rotating seats.** The other 11 presidents share four votes. Each vote lasts one year, and one comes from each of four groups of banks. The new voters take their seats at the first scheduled meeting of the year ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)).
- **Non-voters still speak.** Presidents without a vote "attend the meetings of the Committee, participate in the discussions, and contribute to the Committee's assessment of the economy and policy options" ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)).
- **Alternates.** Each Reserve Bank voter has an alternate, who takes the member's place with the same status when the member is absent. The New York Fed's alternate is its First Vice President ([FOMC Rules of Organization](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/FOMC_RulesOrganization.pdf); [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)).
- **Its own chair.** At its first meeting each year, the FOMC elects a Chair and a Vice Chair from among its members. Its secretary records every vote ([FOMC Rules of Organization](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/FOMC_RulesOrganization.pdf)). In 2026 Kevin Warsh chairs it and John C. Williams, the New York Fed president, is vice chair ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)).

**A real vote.** On 28 October 2026 the FOMC kept its target range at 3¾ to 4% by a 10–2 vote. Two members voted against and wanted a ¼-point rise: Governor Lisa D. Cook, who holds a permanent seat, and Beth M. Hammack, who holds Cleveland's rotating seat in 2026 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)).

### Independence

The Fed's decisions do not need approval from the President or anyone else in the executive or legislative branches. It is still accountable: the Chair testifies to Congress, and the Board sends a Monetary Policy Report twice a year ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/about_12799.htm)).

## The tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Federal funds rate target** | The main tool. The FOMC sets a target for the rate banks charge each other for overnight loans ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)). |
| **Interest on reserve balances** | The Fed pays banks interest on the cash they keep with it. In March 2020 it set this rate at 0.10%, down from 1.10% ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20200315a1.htm)). |
| **Overnight reverse repos** | The Fed borrows cash overnight from eligible firms against Treasury securities it holds ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policytools.htm)). |
| **Open market operations and the balance sheet** | The Fed buys and sells securities. Buying large amounts is quantitative easing (QE), and letting holdings run off is quantitative tightening (QT). The balance sheet went from about $4 trillion to nearly $9 trillion between 2020 and early 2022 ([Richmond Fed](https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/econ_focus/2022/q3_federal_reserve)). |
| **Discount window** | Short-term loans to banks at the discount rate ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policytools.htm)). |
| **Reserve requirements** | Reduced to zero percent from 26 March 2020 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20200315b.htm)). |
| **Forward guidance** | Telling the public the likely future course of policy ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/what-is-forward-guidance-how-is-it-used-in-the-federal-reserve-monetary-policy.htm)). |

## Key takeaways

- The Fed has **two goals**, jobs and stable prices, and is independent in how it pursues them.
- The **FOMC** (12 voters, eight meetings a year) sets the policy rate. Eight seats are permanent: the seven governors and the New York Fed president. The other 11 Reserve Bank presidents take turns in the last four seats, one year at a time.
- **Governors** are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate for staggered 14-year terms. **Reserve Bank presidents** are chosen by their bank's Class B and C directors, with the Board's approval.
- Its tools: the **federal funds rate**, interest on reserves, reverse repos, **QE and QT**, the discount window and **forward guidance**. Reserve requirements are now zero.
