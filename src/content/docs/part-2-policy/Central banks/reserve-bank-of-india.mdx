## What it is

The **Reserve Bank of India** (RBI) was established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. Its central office was first in Kolkata and moved permanently to Mumbai in 1937 ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/AboutUsDisplay.aspx)). Since a 2016 change to that Act, monetary policy has a legal inflation-targeting framework, and the target is 4% with an acceptable range of 2% to 6% ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=17393)). For the goal chart, see the [India page](/Macro-Investing-Textbook/part-1-economy/five-big-economies/india/).

## Who decides

- **The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** has six members. Three are from the RBI: the Governor as chair, the Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy, and one other officer. Three are outside experts appointed by the government ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/Annualpolicy.aspx)).
- It must meet at least four times a year ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/Annualpolicy.aspx)). For example, its 58th meeting ran from 3 to 5 December 2025 ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61749)).

## The tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Repo rate** | The policy rate. It is the rate at which the RBI lends to banks against government securities, through the liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Standing deposit facility** | The rate at which the RBI takes overnight deposits from banks without collateral. It sits 25 basis points below the repo rate ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Marginal standing facility** | An overnight penal borrowing rate, 25 basis points above the repo rate ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Reverse repo rate** | The rate at which the RBI absorbs cash from banks against government securities ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Cash reserve ratio (CRR)** | The share of their deposits that banks must keep with the RBI ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Statutory liquidity ratio (SLR)** | The minimum share of assets banks must hold, usually in government securities ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |
| **Open market operations** | The RBI buys or sells government securities outright to add or remove cash for the longer term ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/FS_Overview.aspx?fn=2752)). |

Two runs stand out. In 2020 the committee cut the rate by 115 basis points during March to May ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1316)), and in 2022 it raised it by a cumulative 250 basis points ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1374)). On 5 December 2025 it cut the rate by 25 basis points to 5.25% ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61749)). The steps are from the RBI's decision statements, listed on its [monetary policy page](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/annualpolicy.aspx).

## Key takeaways

- The RBI has a **legal inflation target** of 4% (2% to 6%).
- A **six-member committee**, three from the RBI and three outsiders, sets the **repo rate** at least four times a year.
- Its toolkit has **rate tools** (repo, standing deposit facility, marginal standing facility) and **balance-sheet tools** (CRR, SLR, open market operations).
